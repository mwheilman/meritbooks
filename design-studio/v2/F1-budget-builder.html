Nora sends targets down, each department head builds up from a template, and the plan moves through controller review to CFO approval with the gap to target in plain view. Owners can work in Excel and upload; the template library recommends lines, drivers and KPIs by industry and by department, and every firm can publish its own.

Stage on the slash meter

Draft · Submitted · Controller · CFO. Filled = done, outlined = waiting on that step, brick = returned. The word always sits beside it.

Owners, not cells

Each department names its owner and template. Owners see only their own departments; approval stays with Erin and Nora, and nobody approves their own.

The gap is the conversation

Plan vs target sits on every row. Sales & marketing is 66,000 over its cap, so Nora’s review opens on that line.

The co-pilot checks, it doesn’t write

The equipment check compares Brent’s draft to posted actuals and sizes the effect. Brent decides.

Recommend, then own

Recommended templates are a start. Copy and edit makes it yours; the firm can publish its set to every client it serves.

Lines come with drivers

Each line has a formula and a source, so a department head plans in leads and trucks, not in account numbers.

KPIs ride along

The template names what the department’s monthly budget-vs-actual will track and when it warns.

Versions, not overwrites

A template edit is a new version. Live plans move only when their owner applies it.

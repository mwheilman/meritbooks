Fourteen clients, one motion

Jordan Smith runs Merit’s move onto MeritBooks from here. A wave is a group of clients moving on the same cutover date, so waves read in date order: Aug 31, Sep 30, Oct 31, then the two clients not scheduled yet. Inside a wave, worst first: a cancelled source outranks everything, then dollars blocked. Every stage tab says what the work is; each row says exactly what’s next as a verb button.

Stages say what the work is

Gather info, Connect systems, Check data quality, Match accounts, Prove balances, Practice close, Signed off, Live. Each tab adds one line of description; hover or focus gives what happens, who does it and when it’s done.

The stage is the brand’s slash

Eight strokes, Gather info to Live. Filled = done, outlined = current, red = current and blocked. Each tab draws the slash its rows carry, so the tabs are the legend.

Waves are cutover dates

Wave 1 Aug 31, Wave 2 Sep 30, Wave 3 Oct 31, then Unscheduled with no cutover and no go-live until the owner names the systems. Wave subtotals and the footer are sums of the blocker column.

Many clients, one motion

Bulk set cutover, assign, request documents, check data quality, apply a mapping set. The QuickBooks loop binds each Intuit company to the right client by EIN before anything is pulled.

Under its own tab

The guide is a table on the same nine-column grid as the tabs, so each stage’s definition sits directly below its count. The first column names the rows.

What happens

One sentence per stage in the client’s terms: cutover, systems, checks, accounts, balances, a month closed twice, a signature, live.

Who does it

Real people at Merit: Jordan Smith plans and settles shared rules, the preparer does the work, a reviewer who never prepared the client signs.

Done when

The exit test for every stage, the same rule the product uses to move a client to the next tab.

# Owner directive — account model, roles, dashboards (Mike, 2026-10-18, verbatim intent)

- I (Mike) am the **system/platform admin**. Platform admins add and manage **tenants** when they sign up
  for the platform (platform admins are not necessarily end users).
- **Tenants** may be:
  - **accounting firms** who in turn have their own **clients** and manage a book of business; or
  - **end-user companies** who have their own bookkeeping staff.
- A tenant may have a **manager who administers the tenant account** — e.g. an accounting manager for a CPA
  firm — who **delegates access to employees to manage the books for their assigned accounts (clients)**.
- Each tenant has **users with role-based access and needs**: owner, CFO, controller, finance director,
  accounting manager, bookkeeper, HR/payroll, operations leader, and any other user.
- **The primary user is the bookkeeper processing the books for the clients.** The system is built to
  automate virtually every function related to that.
- Figure out **what each tier's dashboard looks like and what its function is.**
- **There is no such thing as "portfolio"** — remove those references. **Merit Management is a tenant; its
  portcos are just clients of that tenant.** One of Merit's clients is Merit Management itself, which has
  subsidiaries. As a tenant, Merit may offer the option of **consolidating certain clients so they roll up to
  one report** — and that is true for all clients: if a client is a consolidator, all of the financials in its
  setup can be consolidated.
- **Each client may have a different bookkeeper, so the roll-up view is a permission thing** — each bookkeeper
  is not concerned with the roll-up.
- **Allow me as admin to move things around** on dashboards.
- Visual direction approved: designs **A ("Annual Report")** and **B ("Operator Cockpit")**, approved on visuals
  only, not content. **Do not open the dashboard with a big blue banner with large unnecessary text.** Figure
  out how the dashboard opens so it is more useful and more visually appealing. Content and organization need
  a lot of thought — involve a process agent.
